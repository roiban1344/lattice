This does not mean that an extremal lattice is the densest packing of its dimension: the comparison is only among even unimodular lattices of that dimension, and other lattices, as well as packings that do not come from lattices, are not included. In two cases it is nevertheless true. The extremal lattices $E_8$ and $\Lambda_{24}$ give the densest sphere packings of all in dimensions $8$ and $24$. Viazovska proved this in dimension $8$ [[Via17]](#ref-Via17), and Cohn, Kumar, Miller, Radchenko and Viazovska in dimension $24$ [[CKMRV17]](#ref-CKMRV17).

In an extremal lattice, the vectors of a fixed norm are also evenly spread over the sphere on which they lie. A finite set $X$ on a sphere is a **spherical $t$-design** if, for every polynomial $f$ of degree at most $t$, the average of $f$ over $X$ equals its average over the whole sphere. Venkov showed that in an extremal lattice of dimension $n$, for every nonzero norm, the vectors of that norm form a spherical $11$-design, $7$-design or $3$-design according as $n$ leaves remainder $0$, $8$ or $16$ on division by $24$ [[BV01, Cor. 4.1]](#ref-BV01).
