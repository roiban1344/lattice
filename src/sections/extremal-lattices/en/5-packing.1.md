---
title: Sphere packings and spherical designs
---

This section is a side topic. The bound of the previous section is a statement about theta series, but extremal lattices are also of interest for geometric reasons, and we mention two of them here.

The first is sphere packing. Place a ball of radius $\tfrac12\sqrt{\min\Lambda}$ at every point of a lattice. The balls do not overlap, and there is one ball per fundamental cell, whose volume is $\sqrt{\det\Lambda}$. The proportion of space that the balls fill is therefore

$$
\frac{\text{volume of a ball of radius }\tfrac12\sqrt{\min\Lambda}}{\sqrt{\det\Lambda}} .
$$

For unimodular lattices the denominator is $1$. Among even unimodular lattices of a given dimension, a larger minimum therefore means a denser packing, and an extremal lattice gives the densest packing that the bound allows.

Each ball touches as many others as there are vectors of minimal norm. This number is called the **kissing number** of the packing; for extremal lattices it is the last column of the second table above. The figure shows the same construction in the plane, where the balls become disks: each disk touches six others, so the kissing number of this packing is $6$.
