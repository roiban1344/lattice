---
title: A bound on the minimum
---

The **minimum** $\min\Lambda$ of a lattice is the smallest norm of a nonzero vector. For an even lattice it is an even number. How large can the minimum of an even unimodular lattice be?

The space of modular forms of weight $n/2$ has small dimension, and a basis for it can be built from the lattices we have already met. The orthogonal sum of $a$ copies of $E_8$ and $b$ copies of $\Lambda_{24}$ is an even unimodular lattice of dimension $8a+24b$, and its theta series is $\theta_{E_8}^{\,a}\,\theta_{\Lambda_{24}}^{\,b}$. When $n$ is a multiple of $8$, the products with $8a+24b=n$, one for each $b=0,1,\dots,\lfloor n/24\rfloor$, form a basis of the modular forms of weight $n/2$ [[Ser73, Ch. VII, §3]](#ref-Ser73). So the theta series of every even unimodular lattice of dimension $n$ is a linear combination of these $\lfloor n/24\rfloor+1$ products.
