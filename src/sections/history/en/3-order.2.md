The long delays occur where the bound $2\lfloor n/24\rfloor+2$ has just risen. Dimension $72$ is the first that requires minimum $8$, and it came after dimension $80$. Dimension $96$ is the first that requires minimum $10$, and no example is known. Dimension $48$, the first that requires minimum $6$, is the exception: it was settled early, by the construction from ternary codes. Jenkins and Rouse remark that dimensions divisible by $24$ are particularly challenging [[JR11]](#ref-JR11).

The [page on dimension $112$](../why-112/) uses the mass formula to explain why a larger minimum is harder to reach, and why dimension $112$ was a more promising place to look than $96$ and $104$, although all three require minimum $10$.
